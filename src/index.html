This page authors nothing: every state below was computed by the tracker it names, and is shown in that tracker's own words with that tracker's own printed rule attached. What this page adds is arrangement, and one arithmetic the trackers cannot do alone, which is putting their clocks in order.

The order the clocks ring in

A lead is positive, a lag is negative. Four of these tables publish a lag rather than a lead, and their published figure is negated here to put every clock on one axis; the table prints both numbers so the conversion is auditable. Each row is the median across that tracker's own credited episodes, computed by that tracker, not here.

These medians come from samples that are not comparable in size or era: … against a survey that only starts in 1990, on definitions of an episode that each tracker chose for its own series. The ordering is a description of what these twelve tables contain, not a schedule, and a median of four episodes is not a forecast of the fifth.

What survives those caveats is a shape. The turning points come first and the thresholds come later: housing's crest, freight's crest and the consumer's confidence peak all sit more than a year ahead, the curve and credit's complacency trough about a year ahead, and everything from lending's alarm downward arrives at or after the recession itself. Production's crest sits at exactly … months, which is the hinge: industrial production is one of the series the NBER's committee reads to date a recession, so it does not anticipate the cycle, it constitutes it. Below that line every clock is confirming something already underway.

How to read this page

  • Nothing here is authored. Every state, figure and rule on the board was computed by the tracker it is attributed to and is carried across verbatim. If this page and a tracker ever disagree, the tracker is right and this page has a bug.
  • Two transforms happen here and nowhere else. Overlay series are resampled to monthly, keeping the last observation in each month, because a daily spread and a quarterly survey cannot otherwise share an axis. And the z-score or percentile is computed in your browser from the raw monthly observations in the payload, never stored, because the raw series is the record and a normalized one is a view of it.
  • A z-score against a trailing ten years is a statement about recent history, not about levels. Industrial production and federal debt both rise across decades; scoring them this way asks whether they are unusual for the last ten years, which is the right question for a cycle board and the wrong one for comparing levels. The trackers are where levels are read.
  • There is no composite score on this page and there will not be one. Ten honest indicators averaged into a single number would be the most quotable and least defensible thing here, and it would hide exactly the disagreements the board exists to show.
  • A tracker that cannot be reached keeps its last known row, marked stale and dated to its own last observation. A stale reading is still a true reading about its own date; presenting it as today's would not be.

The ten trackers, and the contract underneath them

    Plumbing

    • Each tracker publishes analysis.status.headline: a binary state, a label, the figures behind it, the observation date it was computed from, and the printed rule. That block is this page's entire interface; it reads nothing else about the page that produced it, which is why a tracker can be rewritten without touching this one.
    • The clock map, which says where each tracker's median lead lives in its payload and what that clock is called, is hand-declared here with a note per entry. Every tracker names its own clock (crest, trough, alarm, slump, confidence peak), so inferring which statistic means "lead" would eventually pick the wrong one.
    • This page fetches no upstream data source and holds no API key. It reads the ten trackers over the internal network on a daily schedule, after each of them has refreshed.
    • Series, provenance and revision policy all follow the shared econ-core contract.
    • Raw data: /api/data.
